ADAS calibration

Driver-assistance systems.
Aligned to spec.

Some vehicles require camera or sensor calibration after windshield replacement or related work. The required method and service availability depend on the exact vehicle and manufacturer procedure.

Why calibration matters

A camera’s view can change when glass changes.

Driver-assistance features may rely on cameras or sensors mounted near the windshield. When service affects their position or view, the vehicle maker may require calibration before the system can operate as intended. A warning light going out by itself does not confirm that calibration is complete.

Vehicle-specific procedures

Static or dynamic?
Follow the spec.

The manufacturer’s service information determines which procedure applies. Some vehicles or repairs may require a combination or another method.

01

Static calibration

Performed with the vehicle stationary using specified targets, positioning, environment, and diagnostic equipment when the vehicle maker calls for it.

02

Dynamic calibration

Performed under vehicle-maker instructions that may involve driving conditions, route, speed, and diagnostic steps when specified for that vehicle.

03

Combined or other procedure

Some vehicles require more than one procedure or additional checks. Always confirm the exact method, prerequisites, and required records for the year, make, model, and trim.

Before requesting an estimate

Details that help confirm the right process

  • Vehicle year, make, model, and trim
  • Which glass is being repaired or replaced
  • Known driver-assistance cameras or sensors
  • Any dashboard warning or recent related work
  • Your ZIP code and preferred timing

Calibration needs, procedures, and availability are vehicle-specific. Ask what the manufacturer requires and whether documentation will be provided for your vehicle.
